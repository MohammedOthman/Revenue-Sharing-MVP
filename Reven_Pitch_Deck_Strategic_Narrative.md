## 1. How to build a pitch deck with a deep strategic narrative

Most decks are a table of contents with slides attached. A strategic narrative is a single argument
about how the world changed, told so that the company's existence is the conclusion rather than the
premise. Five things make the difference.

### 1.1 Use Sequoia's *live* structure, not the template you've seen

Sequoia publishes no template file. Its live guide, **“Writing a Business Plan”** (published
15 March 2019, last modified 13 March 2025), is ten sections of prose:

> Company purpose · Problem · Solution · Why now? · Market potential · Competition / alternatives ·
> Business model · Team · Financials · Vision

Two differences from the version that circulates as “the Sequoia template” matter:

- **There is no Product section and no Traction section.** Product lives inside Solution.
- **There is a Vision closer** — *“If all goes well, what will you have built in five years?”* That is
  written permission to end on the long-range ambition, which otherwise reads as scope creep.

The widely shared 15-slide deck is Malcolm Lewis's 2015 PitchDeckCoach reproduction on SlideShare
(3,389,726 views). Sequoia's own page links to it. So it is fair to use — just don't present it as a
secret, and don't claim Sequoia issued it.

Sequoia's own wording is worth obeying literally:

| Section | Sequoia's instruction |
|---|---|
| Company purpose | “Define your company in a single declarative sentence. This is harder than it looks.” |
| Why now? | “Nature hates a vacuum — so why hasn't your solution been built before now?” |
| Market potential | “One thing **not** to do is to put up huge numbers from some market study without any details behind them.” |
| Competition | “Show that you have a plan to win.” |
| Financials | “If you have any, please include.” |
| Vision | “If all goes well, what will you have built in five years?” |

That Financials line is the licence a pre-revenue company needs: spend the page on **evidence**
instead of a fabricated five-year model.

### 1.2 Open on the change, not the problem

Sequoia's presentation guidance (“How to Present to Investors”) prescribes three slides in the first
five minutes — **What's changed?**, **What you do**, **Fast facts** — then an agenda, then *ask the
room what concerns to address*. Whole presentation in 20 minutes; the rest is discussion.

Note what that ordering does: it puts the **shift before the problem**. Andy Raskin's strategic
narrative says the same and explains why:

> *“When you assert that your prospects have a problem, you put them on the defensive. They may be
> unaware of the problem, or uncomfortable admitting they suffer from it.”*

This matters enormously for Reven. The buyer is a finance leader who will not readily concede that
their reconciliation is broken. Open on ZATCA changing the law, and nobody has to admit anything.

### 1.3 Run Raskin's five beats as the spine

In precisely this order:

1. **Name a big, relevant change in the world.**
2. **Show there'll be winners and losers** — the stakes, both halves.
3. **Tease the Promised Land** — desirable, and hard to reach without help. Describe the *state of the
   world*, not the product. If the sentence contains “platform” or “ledger,” rewrite it.
4. **Introduce capabilities as the “magic gifts”** that overcome the obstacles — titled by the
   obstacle, never by the feature name.
5. **Present evidence you can make the story come true.**

The two frameworks are compatible because Sequoia already sequences shift-first. One deck satisfies
both.

### 1.4 Diagnose the market honestly, in the investor's own vocabulary

Sequoia's **Arc PMF framework** names three archetypes: *Hair on Fire* (urgent, crowded), *Hard Fact*
(customers “have resigned themselves to just living with the problem”; the obstacle is force of habit;
you must educate before you capture), and *Future Vision* (the obstacle is disbelief).

Reven is textbook **Hard Fact**. Two companies reconciling commissions by email don't experience it as
a problem — they experience it as the weather. Naming the archetype converts “no inbound urgency”
from a weakness into a **diagnosed strategy**, and it justifies a market-education line in the plan.

### 1.5 Mechanics that separate good from average

- **Every headline is an assertion** — a full sentence stating the point, never a topic label
  (“Market”, “Competition”). This is the Minto/consulting action-title convention: use it because it
  forces clarity, not because it is proven to raise money.
- **The finishing test.** Paste the headlines into a blank page and read them alone. If that page is
  not a persuasive argument to someone who has never heard of ZATCA, the deck isn't finished — it
  *will* be forwarded to that person.
- **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs two headlines, it is two slides.
- **Size the market bottom-up and show the arithmetic**, labelling each input *sourced* or *assumed*.
  A top-down number from a research report is the fastest way to lose a careful investor.
- **Never win on every axis of a competition slide.** Concede in writing what competitors are better
  at; it is what makes the one thing you claim believable.
- **Volunteer the number that argues against you.** Bringing the disconfirming figure yourself buys
  more credibility than any supporting one.
- **Two artefacts, one narrative.** A read-alone deck that survives being emailed, and a 12-slide
  20-minute live cut.
- **Write the memo first.** Amazon's practice — *“We don't do PowerPoint… Instead, we write
  narratively structured six-page memos”* — is the right order here. Bilateral-ledger economics and
  ZATCA mechanics do not compress into bullets without losing the argument.

## 2. The narrative spine

### The throughline

> **The money one business owes another for bringing it revenue has stopped being a private
> arrangement between two finance teams and become a document a government clears before it can be
> issued — and no one has built the record it turns into.**

A claim about the world, not about Reven. Already law in Saudi Arabia, on a published schedule, and
arriving in the EU, the UAE, India and Brazil on dated timetables.

### The promised land, in one repeatable line

> **“Two companies, one number, no argument.”**

It appears three times: as the purpose (slide 1), as the promised land (slide 7), as the standard
(slide 20). It is the sentence you want repeated after you leave the room.

### The old world / new world

> Partner money used to be a spreadsheet between two finance teams. It is now a filing.
> Nobody's system of record moved.

20.** Coining a category early confuses buyers more often than it creates one.

## 3. The deck, slide by slide

Speaker notes are also embedded in the `.pptx` notes pane.

### 1 · Company purpose
**Headline:** Reven is the system of record for the money one business owes another for bringing it revenue.
**Say:** the sentence, then stop. Don't add features. The pull-quote on the right is the promised land.

### 2 · Fast facts
**Headline:** Reven: [N] people in Riyadh, Phase 1 in build, pre-revenue, raising [amount] for [N] months to reach [milestone].
**Say:** “Before I go further — the facts, so you can weigh everything that follows.” Say *pre-revenue*
plainly; hiding the stage reads as evasion.

### 3 · What's changed  ·  *Raskin beat 1*
**Headline:** In Saudi Arabia the commission invoice stopped being a PDF between two companies and became a document the tax authority clears before it can be issued — and the threshold has fallen 8,000-fold in three and a half years.
**Evidence:** ZATCA Phase 1 (generation) 4 Dec 2021; Phase 2 (integration) 1 Jan 2023, with standard
B2B invoices **cleared before issuance**. Wave 1 caught taxpayers above **SAR 3bn**; **Wave 24** catches
everyone above **SAR 375,000** — the mandatory VAT-registration threshold — announced 26 Sep 2025,
integration by **30 Jun 2026**. **Wave 25** goes to **SAR 187,500** by **1 Feb 2027**. Type codes: 388
invoice, 381 credit note, 383 debit note — so a clawback and a true-up are each cleared filings.
**Say:** “I want to start with something that has already happened, not something I'm predicting… A
commission is an invoice. That invoice is now a cleared government document, and partner economics are
inherently retroactive — which means every clawback is a credit note the regulator sees.”

### 4 · Winners and losers  ·  *Raskin beat 2*
**Headline:** The company that can characterise, evidence and file every partner payment closes its month. The company that cannot is missing a filing deadline by hand, at 1% a month, forever.
**Evidence:** Income Tax Law Art. 68 — withholding **5%–20%** on payments to non-residents. Management
fees 20%; royalties 15%; technical/consulting **5% unrelated, 15% related**; rent/freight/air/telecoms
5%. VAT 15% on the commission leg, which under ZATCA's Agents Guideline is a **separate supply** for a
*disclosed* agent (an *undisclosed* agent is generally treated as principal instead). Returns due by
the **10th of the following month**; **1% per 30 days**, simple and uncapped.
**Say:** “The rate isn't one number — it's five, fifteen or twenty depending on what the payment *is*,
and it triples from five to fifteen if the counterparty is related to you. That's a data model. It is
not a column in a spreadsheet.”

### 5 · The problem
**Headline:** Two companies, two ledgers, one payment — and neither version is the one a regulator or an auditor will accept.
**Evidence:** The failure is *bilateral*. IFRS 15 B34–B38 / ASC 606 decide gross-vs-net on a
principal-versus-agent control test, and the evidence for that judgement is attribution fact living in
spreadsheets. Under ASC 340-40 the commission is a **capitalised asset**, amortised over the expected
customer life — audited data, which is why the buyer is the controller. And: **no research house
publishes a total for B2B partner commissions, revenue shares, rebates and referral fees as a single
category.** The money is unmeasured because it is unowned.
**Say:** “When we say system of record, we mean the record an auditor accepts, not a dashboard.”

### 6 · Why nobody fixed it
**Headline:** The incumbent is the spreadsheet — and the reason no partner-software vendor displaced it is that the category was defined to stop before the money.
**Evidence:** G2's six PRM inclusion criteria (portals; onboarding/certification; access management;
workflows; deal registration; MDF effectiveness) contain **no payout, no invoicing, no tax, no
reconciliation, no ledger**. G2's own words: *“While some PRM solutions may also track compliance with
laws, contractual agreements, and incentive and loyalty programs, these are typically considered as
secondary capabilities.”* Where payout does appear it is **Rebate Management** — supplier→reseller, one
direction, outside the partnerships tree.
**Say:** “This is not our claim about our competitors. It's the independent review marketplace's
definition of what our competitors *are*.”

### 7 · The promised land  ·  *Raskin beat 3*
**Headline:** Both companies close the month against one number neither has to argue about — and it is the same number the tax authority already cleared.
No data. A sentence and a picture.

### 8 · The core insight
**Headline:** A ledger only one side trusts is just an invoice. The durable asset is one mutually approved ruleset both counterparties settle against — and the regulator has already made the settlement document itself shared.
**Say:** “The eureka is that we don't have to invent a shared record — the regulator built one. What
ZATCA holds is that an invoice was issued at a rate. What it does *not* hold is why: who sourced the
deal, under what protection window, whether the claim was eligible under which version of the
agreement, whether the counterparty is related. Those facts determine the amount and the rate, and
they exist nowhere but in email.”

### 9 · Product today — Phase 1, Capture
**Headline:** Phase 1 delivers complete value to one company on day one, requires zero consent from the counterparty — and captures exactly the data the settlement layer will need.
**Ships:** partner registry · claim registration · attribution-of-record · protection windows ·
eligibility preview · evidence packs · tax-field capture · append-only ledger (recording states, not
executing them). **No money movement.**
**Precedent:** Crossbeam's account mapping scaled because each side uploads only its own list and only
the intersection is revealed — 25,000+ companies by 2024, 30,000+ today.
> **Replace this slide with three real screenshots before presenting.**

### 10 · The magic gifts  ·  *Raskin beat 4*
**Headline:** Every capability exists to remove one specific thing standing between a finance team and a closed month.

| The obstacle | The gift |
|---|---|
| “Finance rebuilds the partner statement by hand every month.” | Evidence pack |
| “Nobody agrees who sourced the deal, and the argument happens after the money is due.” | Attribution-of-record + protection windows |
| “Is this 5%, 15% or 20% — and is this counterparty related to us?” | Tax-field capture at claim time |
| “A clawback is a phone call, and then a fight.” | Credit/debit note handling (381 / 383) |

### 11 · The roadmap as a strategic argument
**Headline:** You cannot settle what you never captured, and you cannot orchestrate a network with no settled truth — so the money layer is earned in sequence.

| | **Capture** (0–9 mo) | **Settle** (9–24 mo) | **Orchestrate** (24 mo+) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Claim-centric PRM. No money movement. | Rule engine + append-only bilateral ledger; both sides settle against one approved ruleset | Cross-company network, partner P&L, forecasting, co-sell, bps on settled flow |
| **Why it must come first** | The only phase delivering full value with **zero counterparty consent** | A ledger with no captured claims has nothing to reconcile | A network whose members dispute last quarter is a directory |
| **What it earns** | The attribution record — the fact base for the rate | The right to charge on flow through our record | Network economies — a late-stage power |
| **Kill criterion** | Capture must sell standalone at target ACV before we build Settle | A counterparty pair must sign one ruleset before we build Orchestrate | — |

**The precedent block — money-second is the observed shape, not our theory:**

| | Revenue mix | Take on flow |
|---|---|---|
| **Toast** FY2024 | fintech **$4,053M** vs subscription **$706M** of $4,960M — **81.7%**, 5.7× | **2.55% gross** on $159.1bn GPV; **55 bps net** at 21.7% margin |
| **ServiceTitan** LTM Jul-24 | subscription **~70%**, usage-based ~25% | **~0.25% of GTV** |
| **Shopify** FY2024 ⚠ | merchant ~$6.53B vs subscription ~$2.35B of $8.88B — **~73%** | — (verify at filing) |

**Say:** “We could have built the ledger first. We didn't — because a ledger with no captured claims
has nothing to settle, and a network with no settled ledger has nothing to orchestrate. Toast,
ServiceTitan and Shopify all did the record first and the money second. None of them did it the other
way round. And ServiceTitan is *still* seventy percent subscription — the flow layer is an addition,
not a replacement.”

### 12 · Why now (I) — the regulatory clock
**Headline:** This is not a market we have to create. It is a deadline that exists whether or not we do — and Saudi Arabia reaches it about three years before the EU.
**Evidence:** KSA Wave 24 (30 Jun 2026) and Wave 25 (1 Feb 2027). **ViDA** adopted as Council Directive
(EU) 2025/516 on 11 Mar 2025, in force 14 Apr 2025 — intra-EU structured e-invoicing plus
near-real-time reporting from **1 Jul 2030**, full convergence by **1 Jan 2035**. Germany receipt Jan
2025 → issuance Jan 2027 (>€800k) → all Jan 2028; Belgium Jan 2026; Poland Feb/Apr 2026; France
receive-all Sep 2026, SMEs Sep 2027. UAE: Peppol **five-corner** on PINT AE — ASP appointment 31 Jul
2026, mandatory 1 Jan 2027 (≥AED 50m), 1 Jul 2027 below. **Saudi runs clearance; the UAE runs Peppol.
Two neighbours, two incompatible stacks.**
**The counter-example, volunteered:** the US moved the *other* way — OBBBA (2025) restored the 1099-K
threshold to $20,000 / 200+ transactions, retroactive to 2022. The US is the outlier, not the model.

### 13 · Why now (II) — the economic forces
**Headline:** Direct acquisition got expensive at exactly the moment the channels that fed it stopped working — so growth migrated to revenue that arrives through somebody else, and every one of those creates a bilateral money obligation.
**Evidence:** **$2.00** of S&M per $1.00 of new ARR in 2024 (up 14%) against a **$1.00** Expansion CAC
ratio (Benchmarkit). Pew (900 US adults, 68,879 searches, Mar 2025): **8%** click-through when an AI
Overview appears vs **15%** without; **1%** on the summary's own links. Hyperscaler marketplace software
**$30bn (2024) → $163bn (2030)**, 29.1% CAGR, partners facilitating nearly 60% of transactions. **6.3
partners per customer** — reconciliation cost scales with counterparties, not revenue. And between Apr
2025 and Jun 2026 the rails for machine commerce were standardised (Mastercard Agent Pay, Visa
Intelligent Commerce, Google AP2 with 60+ organisations, OpenAI/Stripe's Agentic Commerce Protocol) —
**not one of them answers who gets credit for the sale.**
**The number that argues against us:** partner share of the addressable IT market falls to **66.7% in
2026 from 70.1%** — because AI capex inflates the direct denominator. Partner-delivered spend still
grew 6.7% on a $6.07tn base.

### 14 · Market, built bottom-up
**Headline:** We size this by counting the Saudi businesses inside the mandate and multiplying by what we charge — because the category-level number does not exist.
**The ladder:** `1.91M active commercial registrations` × `% inside a live ZATCA wave` × `% running a
partner programme` × `% reachable` × `ACV`. Rungs 1–2 sourced; rungs 3–5 assumed, labelled as such on
the slide. Supporting: 35.2% of new SME registrations in Q2 2025 (28,181) were in Riyadh Province;
259,000 new commercial registrations Kingdom-wide in 2025; MISA issued 15,728 investment licences in
9M-2025 vs 14,320 in all of 2024; MENA IT spend $169bn in 2026 (+8.9%), software $20.4bn (+13.9%).
**Say:** “Every input in blue has a source on this page. Every input in amber is an assumption I'm
making, and I'll defend each one. The one I'm least certain of is the third rung.”

### 15 · Competition and alternatives
**Headline:** Our real competitor is a spreadsheet and an emailed PDF. The software competitors are genuinely better at partner marketing — and are consolidating into one commerce platform that structurally cannot be neutral.
**We concede, in writing:** PartnerStack, Impartner and Crossbeam are better than us at partner
recruitment, partner marketing and ecosystem data, and will be for years.
**The structural claim:** AppDirect acquired **Tackle.io (Dec 2025)** and **PartnerStack (Apr 2026,
reported “upwards of $150m”)** — get-found, get-chosen, get-paid under one owner. It **settles as the
marketplace**, taking a cut of the transaction it would be adjudicating. A party with an economic
interest in the amount cannot be the record both counterparties trust.
**Price follows the record:** Crossbeam publishes $4,800/yr + $1,800/seat, Supernode from $25,000/yr —
for a product that deliberately does not touch money. Salesforce PRM: $25/member/month or $10/login.

### 16 · Business model
**Headline:** We charge a subscription for the record and refuse a take rate on partner money — and when the settlement layer ships, the flow fee is tens of basis points, not percent.
**Evidence:** Toast gross take **2.55%** → **55 bps net** at 21.7% margin. ServiceTitan **~25 bps** of
GTV with subscription still ~70% of revenue. Stripe Billing's published **0.70%** is the checkable
ceiling. Retention underwritten at **100–105% base / 115% bull**, gross retention as the floor (median
SaaS NRR ~101%, Benchmarkit 2025).
**Say:** “Any founder who shows you two percent of a big number hasn't read the filings.”

### 17 · Evidence  ·  *Raskin beat 5*
**Headline:** We have no revenue. We have [N] dated, countable, falsifiable pieces of evidence that this is real — here they are.
Design partners · signed LOIs / **priced** pilots · discovery-interview count **and the unprompted
hit rate** · regulatory/tax-advisor validation · a live demo link. Every row dated.
> 🔴 **The largest hole in the deck, and entirely founder-supplied.** If it cannot be filled with
> dated, checkable items, do not raise yet.

### 18 · Team
**Headline:** Three unsolved parts of this problem. One of us for each.
Columns lead with the *unsolved problem* — the regulatory judgement, the bilateral finance operation,
the ledger — and only then the person. First two hires the plan funds: a ledger/payments-infrastructure
engineer and a KSA tax/compliance specialist.

### 19 · The ask
**Headline:** We are raising [amount] for [N] months to hit three dated milestones that de-risk the Series A — not to ship three features.
1. *N* paying Capture customers from the Wave 24/25 cohort → **de-risks: will anyone pay for the record alone?**
2. One bilateral pilot where **both** counterparties approve the same ruleset → **de-risks: the entire Phase 2 thesis.**
3. ZATCA-cleared credit and debit notes in production → **de-risks: can we operate inside the mandate?**

### 20 · Vision
**Headline:** In five years, two companies in different countries settle a partner payout against one record that both their auditors and both their tax authorities accept.
The only slide that names the category. **Say:** “Somebody is going to build that record. The only real
question is whose ledger both sides sign.”
