# Reven — Pricing & Tiers: Executive Summary

**Reven** is the enterprise, finance-grade **control layer for partner revenue** — the system of record for the revenue-share claim, attribution, eligibility, statements, and settlement, across **every industry with partnership and revenue-share economics**.

**This document is the canonical pricing model.** It supersedes the per-active-partner model previously described here and in the wider pricing corpus. Figures in **SAR** (KSA contracts in SAR; VAT 15%; USD peg 3.75 for reference only).

---

## 1. The model, in one line

> **SAR 50 per seat, per month.** One price, published, self-serve. It is a deliberate **land-and-expand** entry — not a value-based price — and the expansion is into **enterprise contracts sold on the partnership intelligence layers, once those layers ship.**

**SAR 50/seat/month = SAR 600/seat/year ≈ USD 160/seat/year.**

### The two stages

| | **Stage 1 — Land (today)** | **Stage 2 — Expand (earned)** |
|---|---|---|
| **What's sold** | The core record: partner registry, claims, attribution-of-record, evidence, statements | Partnership intelligence: portfolio capacity, cross-company coordination, outcome verification, benchmarking, governance & integration |
| **Pricing metric** | **Seats** | Enterprise contract — **not yet set** |
| **Price** | **SAR 50/seat/month** | **TBD** *(modelled band only — see §3)* |
| **Motion** | Self-serve, zero-touch | Executive sale, finance as economic buyer |
| **Job** | Get inside the account; become where the record lives | Capture the value |

### Three metrics, kept deliberately separate

| | Definition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Value metric** | Trusted revenue-share flow governed | What the customer actually buys; the North Star |
| **Pricing metric** | **Paid seats** (Stage 1) → enterprise contract (Stage 2) | Seats are frictionless to buy and honest to meter; they do not attempt to capture value |
| **Expansion metric** | seats → functions → entities → **intelligence layers** → compliance tier → countries | The NRR engine. **Layers, not seats, carry the expansion** |

## 2. Stage 1 — the seat price

**One published price, every customer, every sector: SAR 50 per seat per month.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Billing** | Monthly or annual; annual prepay preferred (beats the GCC cash cycle) |
| **Minimum** | None. A team of 8 pays SAR 400/month |
| **Motion** | **Fully self-serve.** No implementation, no CSM, AI/community support |
| **Published?** | **Yes** — the price is public and unqualified |

**Why a single flat seat price rather than tiers.** Tiered per-partner pricing forced a discovery conversation before the customer had any value, and it penalised exactly the multi-partner accounts we most want. A flat seat price removes the negotiation, removes procurement, and makes the product's usefulness — not its packaging — the thing that determines revenue.

**Local calibration.** SAR 50/month sits just below the local micro-SaaS floor (Marn ~56, Salla/Matajer ~99, ≈ one Zoho CRM seat ~53), so it reads as obviously affordable in-market while leaving headroom to ~SAR 99–149/seat/month if seat value proves out.

> **Guardrails, because zero-touch economics are unforgiving.** At SAR 600/seat/year, **one human support call can erase a small account's annual revenue.** Stage 1 must stay genuinely self-serve: no implementation, no named CSM, no bespoke onboarding. The kill metric is **land-to-expand rate**, not Stage-1 margin — Stage 1 is not expected to be profitable on its own.

## 4. Commercial model

- **No take rate. Ever.** Reven never charges a percentage of the money flowing between counterparties (the model impact.com ~2.5% and PartnerStack-style take-fees use, and the one finance buyers most resent). This is **positional consistency**, not squeamishness: Reven's product is a number two companies both accept, and a referee taking a cut of the score is not a referee. It also keeps Reven valued as software rather than as payments.
- **Settlement fees, when settlement ships:** flat per-payout, separately reported — never a percentage of the payout.
- **Sharia structuring:** any later flow-linked fee is structured as Ju'ala/Wakala — a fee for a defined service, not a share of another party's revenue. The same idea in a different legal tradition.
- **Implementation:** none at Stage 1 (that is the point). At Stage 2, fixed-fee + milestone, **fenced below 20% of revenue**, pushed to local SI partners over time.
- **Compliance ladder — the GCC wedge incumbents can't retrofit:** L0 Standard → **L1** ZATCA/WHT/PDPL → **L2** in-Kingdom residency / dedicated tenancy (**+15–30%**) → **L3** Sharia-certified. Priced at Stage 2 only.
- **Contracting:** monthly or annual at Stage 1; annual default with multi-year for enterprise/government at Stage 2, with a fixed-% escalation floor and auto-renew. **Milestone disbursement for semi-government** (which pays in arrears).
- **Discount guardrails (Stage 2 only — Stage 1 is never discounted):** governed on **pocket price** net of channel/SI margin. **≤25% default**; up to ~30% strategic with give-gets. Hard floor: **≥70% blended gross margin.**

---

## 5. Why this structure

1. **It removes every reason to say no.** At SAR 400/month for a team, there is no business case, no committee, and no procurement cycle. Adoption is the only variable.
2. **It refuses the most-resented model** — the visible take-rate on partner money. Refusing it early *is* the neutral-Switzerland wedge that lets Reven sit between two counterparties.
3. **It puts the expansion where the value actually is.** Seats cannot reach enterprise revenue — SAR 600/seat/year would need ~547 seats in one company. **Only the intelligence layers can**, which is why they are the roadmap priority rather than an upsell afterthought.
4. **It fits every sector.** A flat seat price needs no per-industry packaging, so insurance, franchising, logistics and construction buy the same way — essential for a horizontal product.
5. **It protects the multiple.** Fixed recurring software with a capped, separately-reported settlement layer is valued as software.

---

## 6. The one number to watch

> **Land-to-expand rate: the share of seat accounts that convert to an enterprise contract within four quarters.**

Everything in this model depends on it. Below ~15% the seat price is too low and must rise; above ~20% the expansion thesis is proven and the seat price can go lower still to widen the funnel. Instrument it from customer one.
