# Lens 07 — Sell the consequence, not the saving

| | |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Go-to-market |
| **Decides** | The order of the sales narrative — what opens, what closes |
| **Argues against** | Leading with leakage recovery |
| **One line** | Penalties move budgets faster than savings, and the corpus currently leads with the weakest of four consequences |

---

## The frame

Enterprise software is bought against **consequences**, not against benefits. The question a buyer is actually answering is *"what happens to me if I do nothing?"*

Reven prevents four distinct consequences. They are not equally urgent, and the difference is large.

---

## Rationale

| Rank | Consequence prevented | Owner | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **ZATCA / WHT filing error** → assessment, penalty, repeat exposure | Finance / Tax | **Non-discretionary, dated, externally enforced — and sector-blind** |
| **2** | **PDPL or sector-regulator breach** → up to SAR 5M, doubling on repeat, criminal exposure for intentional disclosure | Legal / Compliance | **Live case record** (~48 decisions by early 2026); personal exposure; sharper in insurance, health and banking |
| **3** | **Counterparty dispute** → damaged or lost distribution relationship | Commercial owner | Painful, but usually resolvable socially — **except in franchising, where it goes legal** |
| **4** | **Leakage / overpayment** → 3–8% of payout | Finance | Real money — but **already lost, and long since normalised** |

### Why #4 is the weakest, despite being the biggest number

Leakage is the most quantifiable of the four, which is why it is tempting to lead with. It is also the least urgent, for a specific reason:

> **Leakage is money that has already gone, every year, for years — and has been absorbed into the cost base as normal.**

Nobody is being asked about it. No deadline attaches to it. No regulator is enforcing it. A CFO who has tolerated a 5% payout error for a decade can tolerate it for another quarter, and will, if the alternative is a procurement cycle.

### Why #1 is the strongest, despite being a smaller number

A filing error is:

- **Externally enforced** — the buyer does not get to decide whether it matters.
- **Dated** — Wave 25 lands 1 February 2027.
- **Non-discretionary** — it comes from a budget that survives cuts.
- **Sector-blind** — the same pitch works in insurance, franchising, logistics and construction without translation.

**A penalty avoided beats a saving claimed**, because the penalty has a date and an enforcer, and the saving has neither.

---

## What it changes

> ### Lead with #1, support with #2, close with #4.

Concretely:

- **Opening message:** the Wave 25 deadline and cross-border WHT exposure on intermediary payments. Not leakage.
- **Second conversation, once legal is in the room:** PDPL — *"how do you reconcile commission today?"* → *"we exchange spreadsheets"* → *"under PDPL, who authorised that transfer of policyholder data?"* This reaches a **third budget owner** beyond finance and the commercial team.
- **Business case, once you are already in the room:** leakage. It is a superb ROI justification and a poor door-opener. Underwrite the **conservative 3–8% band**, not the aggressive 10–30% — a claim you beat is worth more than a claim you defend.
- **Never lead with #3 externally.** Disputes are the strongest *retention* argument — a dispute resolved inside Reven is a customer who cannot leave — and a weak *acquisition* argument, because admitting to disputes is admitting to a management failure.

---

## The argument against this lens

Compliance-led positioning risks Reven being **bought as a compliance tool and shelved once the deadline passes** — a one-time purchase rather than a system of record.

**Resolution:** this is a real risk, and it is exactly why the sequence matters rather than just the opening. The compliance consequence opens the door; the **evidence pack and the accumulated agreement** ([Lens 02](02-trust-is-the-product.md)) are what make it permanent. If the product delivers only compliance, the criticism is correct — and the answer is product, not messaging.
