# Lens 08 — Sell an artifact, not an architecture

| | |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Positioning |
| **Decides** | The words in the first sentence of every pitch |
| **Argues against** | Leading with "OS," "control layer," or "system of record" |
| **One line** | Categories are built from a repeated artifact, not a repeated adjective |

---

## The frame

The corpus already decided to keep **"orchestrator"** as an internal north star rather than an external banner. That decision is right.

**Go one step further.** In Phase 1, do not lead with **"OS," "control layer,"** or even **"system of record"** either.

Lead with the thing the customer receives:

> ### "The revenue-share statement both sides sign."

---

## Rationale

### Why architecture words hurt at this stage

Architecture words invite **platform-scale scrutiny that a pre-seed company cannot survive.** Say "we're building an OS" and the next four questions are:

- *Show me the roadmap.*
- *Show me the integration surface.*
- *Show me the team that builds a platform.*
- *What happens when Salesforce ships this?*

Every one of those is a question about **capacity**, and capacity is the weakest thing a pre-seed company has. The words invite the audit the company is least ready for.

### Why the artifact works instead

"The revenue-share statement both sides sign" is:

- **Concrete** — it is a document, not a category.
- **Demonstrable in one screen** — the demo is the proof.
- **Immediately understood by finance** — no translation, no category education.
- **Impossible to confuse with a PRM** — no PRM produces a mutually-signed statement.
- **Small enough to be believed** from a pre-seed team.

### The multi-sector property that makes this decisive

The artifact **translates into every sector's vocabulary without changing meaning**:

| Sector | What they call it |
|---|---|
| Insurance | The commission statement |
| Franchising | The royalty statement |
| Logistics / travel | The agent settlement |
| Construction | The subcontractor payment certificate |
| Technology | The partner payout statement |

**One object, five names, zero repositioning.** An architecture word does not travel like this — "control layer" means nothing to a franchise director, and "PRM" actively misleads an insurance broker. The artifact is the only framing that survives the multi-sector scope intact.

### The category-building mechanism

> **Categories are built from a repeated artifact, not a repeated adjective.**

Salesforce did not build its category by saying "platform." It built it by making *the pipeline report* a thing every sales organisation produced. Gainsight made *the health score*. Crossbeam made *the overlap*.

The architecture language earns its place **once a hundred of those statements exist** — at which point it is a description of something real rather than a claim about something planned.

---

## What it changes

- **Rewrite the first sentence** of the deck, the website and every outbound message around the artifact.
- **Make the demo the artifact.** Open on a statement and its lineage, not on a dashboard or a funnel. (See [Lens 09](09-evidence-not-workflow.md).)
- **Name the object consistently in product**, with per-sector vocabulary as configuration — the same data model, different labels.
- **Reserve "system of record" for the investor conversation**, where platform ambition is an asset rather than a liability. The buyer and the investor need different words for the same thing, and that is fine.
- **Count statements as a company metric.** "Statements both parties accepted" is the artifact made measurable — and it is the truest proxy for the moat ([Lens 02](02-trust-is-the-product.md)).

---

## The argument against this lens

Leading with a narrow artifact risks **anchoring the price to a document** rather than to a platform — and a document sounds like it should cost less than a system.

**Resolution:** the artifact is the *entry* framing, not the permanent one, and the pricing model already handles this. **SAR 50 per seat is deliberately beneath the threshold where anyone argues about value** ([`../Reven_Pricing_Executive_Summary.md`](../Reven_Pricing_Executive_Summary.md)), so anchoring low at entry costs nothing. The enterprise expansion is priced on the intelligence layers, which are a different conversation with a different buyer.
