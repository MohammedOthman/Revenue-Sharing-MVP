# Trend 01 — Partner-led growth is becoming more important to enterprise revenue

| | |
|---|---|
| **Class** | Market-making — true and large, but equally available to well-funded incumbents |
| **Use it for** | Sizing the prize and validating the category. **Never as differentiation** |
| **Dated clock** | None. This is a direction, not a deadline |
| **One line** | Intermediated revenue is taking share from direct revenue — and has become material enough to be *audited* rather than tolerated |

---

## The claim

More of what enterprises sell now reaches the customer through somebody else — a partner, broker, agent, franchisee, dealer, reseller or platform — rather than through their own direct salesforce.

---

## Rationale — why it is true

Read sector-generally, three structural forces drive this. None is a fashion.

**1. Distribution economics.** Reaching a customer through someone who already holds the relationship is cheaper than building that relationship yourself. This is why insurance sells through brokers, franchises expand through franchisees, and hotels fill rooms through OTAs. It is not a technology-industry insight; technology arrived at it late.

**2. Cost of acquisition.** An intermediary is paid *after* revenue exists. A direct salesforce is paid before. That asymmetry matters more as capital gets expensive, and it is why intermediated models gain share in tight cycles.

**3. Solution complexity.** Outcomes increasingly require several parties, so a transaction has multiple contributors by construction — which pushes work toward partners rather than away from them.

**The evidence, with confidence grades:**

| Evidence | Figure | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise IT transacting through ≥1 channel partner | **~70%** | HIGH |
| Microsoft commercial revenue that is partner-influenced | **~95%** | HIGH |
| Partner revenue earned per $1 Microsoft takes | **~$8.45–10.93** | HIGH/MED |
| Partner-ecosystem leaders planning to increase PRM investment | **69%** | MED |
| KSA insurance gross written premium growth, 2025 (licensed broker/agent channel) | **+10.7%** to SAR 84.3bn | HIGH |
| KSA franchise registrations, growth to Q3 2024 | **+866%** to 1,788 | MED–HIGH |

> **On the technology figures:** they are the best-instrumented evidence, not a statement about Reven's market. The same dynamic runs through insurance brokerage, franchising, travel distribution and freight agency worldwide — those sectors simply publish worse numbers. The KSA rows above are the non-technology check, and they point the same way. The franchise-registration figure is a particularly clean measure, because it counts *intermediated business formation* directly.

---

## Why now

The change is **not** that intermediated revenue got big. In insurance and franchising it always was. The change is that it got **material enough to be audited, in sectors that previously settled on trust.**

Two dated markers sit inside the last twelve months:

- **September 2025** — Gartner retired "Partner Relationship Management (PRM) Applications" and created **"Partner and Ecosystem Relationship Management (PERM)"**, moving the unit of management from a linear channel to a multi-party ecosystem and explicitly framing it as *foundation, not a CRM extension*.
- **December 2025 → April 2026** — capital repriced the space: AppDirect acquired **Tackle.io**, then **PartnerStack** (reported $150M+).

In Saudi Arabia across all sectors, e-invoicing and local-content reporting are simultaneously converting informal commercial relationships into documented ones.

> **The mechanism to remember:** the moment a revenue motion becomes material **and** documented, it moves from *tolerated on spreadsheets* to *audited*. That transition is when a system of record gets bought — and it is happening now.

---

## Strategic implication

**1. It fixes the denominator.** Reven must size against *revenue-share flow under management*, not against the PRM software market. Across all sectors that flow is plausibly **$6–12bn/yr in KSA** [derived, LOW] against a **~$1–3bn global** narrow-PRM software market. The gap between those two numbers is the entire thesis.

**2. Importance creates demand for *evidence*, not for *management*.** This is the distinction the whole positioning turns on. Rising intermediation does not create demand for a PRM — twenty already exist. It creates demand for **a number the business can defend.** The sentence is not "run your partners better." It is:

> **"Prove what your partners produced."**

**3. Problem ownership migrates to finance.** In every sector, once intermediary payments are material, the owner becomes whoever signs off on them. Land with the commercial owner — Head of Partnerships, Head of Distribution, Franchise Director, Head of Agency — and expand to the CFO. Both buy the same artifact for different fears.

---

## Operational implication

- **Instrument revenue-share flow under management** in every pilot, in the customer's own vocabulary — commission, royalty, margin or take rate. It is simultaneously the North Star, the price justification, and the number an investor underwrites.
- **Keep the claim, not the partner profile, as the core object**, so the system is natively an evidence system rather than a directory that grew reports.
- **Make materiality the qualifier**, sector-independently: below ~5% of revenue the pain is real but not urgent, and the deal stalls in procurement.
- **Open discovery with their own intermediated percentage**, then ask the disqualifying question: *"Who signs off on that number today, and what do they check it against?"* **The quality of the silence is the qualification.**

---

## What would falsify it

Intermediated revenue can be **important but tolerated** — growing, visible, and still nobody's audit problem. If discovery finds no finance owner who feels exposed by the number, this trend supports a **reporting tool**, not a system of record, and the price ceiling collapses accordingly.
