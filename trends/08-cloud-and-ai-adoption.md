# Trend 08 — Companies are adopting cloud software and AI at increasing rates

| | |
|---|---|
| **Class** | **Enabling** — a precondition for the market existing, not a segment of it |
| **Use it for** | A **qualifier** in discovery, and for explaining why the pool grows faster than GDP. Not a target |
| **Dated clock** | None |
| **One line** | Read literally this is the narrowest trend on the list; its real value is that digitization is what brings the non-ICT sectors into range at all |

> **Handle with care.** For a multi-sector business, taking this trend at face value would pull the company back toward an ICT-first posture it has deliberately left.

---

## The claim

Enterprises are moving to cloud software and adopting AI, and Saudi Arabia is investing in that capacity at national scale.

---

## Rationale — why it is true

| Evidence | Figure | Confidence |

| KSA public cloud spend | $2.4bn (2024) → **~$4.7bn (2027)**, ~23% CAGR | HIGH |
| KSA enterprise software | $3.2bn (2024) → $6.9bn (2030), **14.3% CAGR** | MED |

## Why now

Three mechanisms. **For a multi-sector business the third is the important one, and it is the one usually missed.**

**1. Cloud and AI are delivered through partners.** Nobody deploys an enterprise platform without a systems integrator, so the capex wave is simultaneously a partner-services wave. *This is what makes the ICT pool grow at 14–23% rather than at GDP rates.*

**2. AI makes attribution harder, not easier.** Consumption pricing means intermediated revenue is no longer a one-time booking but a **metered stream**, with true-ups and reversals arriving months after the contribution. Deal-registration models cannot represent this.

**3. Digitization is what brings the non-ICT sectors into range at all.** An insurance broker network, a franchise system or a freight agency still running on spreadsheets and email **cannot be sold a settlement system of record — there is no data to reconcile.** Cloud adoption across those sectors is the *precondition* for Reven's addressable market existing outside technology.

> **Read this trend as the enabler of the field, not as a segment of it.**

---

## Strategic implication

### On AI as a feature

Gartner and Forrester now treat AI orchestration as **table stakes, not differentiator**. So Reven needs a credible AI story without letting AI become the pitch — **the pitch is trust.**

The differentiating discipline: **sell AI as evidence-preserving, not decision-making.**

- An AI that **proposes** an attribution split and records the human approval is valuable.
- An AI that **decides** one is a liability — because when an auditor asks *"who approved this split?"*, the answer cannot be *"the model."*

Front-office vendors can be cavalier here. A system of record cannot. **And this matters more, not less, in regulated sectors** — an insurance or healthcare regulator will not accept a model as an approver.

### On consumption revenue

The capability to attribute a **stream** — with clawbacks, netting and true-ups — is qualitatively different from registering a deal, and it **generalizes usefully across sectors.** A franchise royalty on rolling gross sales, an insurance trailer commission, and a SaaS consumption stream are the same object with different names. Building it once serves all three.

---

## Operational implication

- **Support recurring, metered, revisable revenue events** — not only closed transactions. Design for true-ups and reversals from the first migration. This serves franchise royalties and trailer commissions as much as consumption billing.
- **Ship exactly three AI features, all advisory:** duplicate and near-duplicate claim detection; attribution suggestion with a confidence score and cited evidence; leakage and anomaly flagging. **Each writes a *suggestion*; a human writes the *fact*.**
- **Meter and cap AI cost per tenant**, fenced from the ≥70% blended gross-margin floor. Uncapped inference against enterprise data volume is how a software margin becomes a services margin.
- **Use sector digitization as a qualifying signal, not a target.** Ask: *"What system are the counterparty payments calculated in today?"* — **"Excel" means no data; "a core system with an export" means a pilot.** This is the single fastest disqualifier available, and it should run in the first discovery call.

---

## What would falsify it

If the chosen sectors' intermediary calculations remain **genuinely offline**, Reven's addressable market outside technology is smaller than the sector sizes imply — the accounts exist but cannot be served.

Separately, if KSA AI spend concentrates in **government mega-projects procured directly**, with a thin commercial partner layer, the denominator grows without the attribution problem growing alongside it. Watch whether the build-out creates a *services channel* or just infrastructure contracts.
