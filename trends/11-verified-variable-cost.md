# Trend 11 — Companies prefer variable commercial costs when they can be clearly measured

| | |
|---|---|
| **Class** | **Mechanism-making** — the economic reason the company exists |
| **Use it for** | The one-sentence thesis, and the argument for the pricing model |
| **Dated clock** | None |
| **One line** | Variable commercial cost scales only as far as its verification does — and Reven is the verification |

---

## The claim

Companies would rather pay for commercial outcomes after they happen than carry fixed cost in advance — **but only when the trigger can be verified.**

---

## Rationale — why it is true

**The shift is the engine underneath intermediated growth.** A commission, royalty or agent fee is paid *after* revenue exists; a salaried salesforce is paid before. That asymmetry is why intermediated models gain share whenever capital is expensive — and why **insurance, franchising, travel distribution and freight agency are built entirely on variable cost.**

**The conditional clause is the entire trend.** Variable cost is attractive only if the trigger can be **verified**:

> **Unverifiable variable cost is *worse* than fixed cost. It is fixed cost plus dispute risk plus audit exposure.**

Franchising states this most clearly of any sector: a royalty on gross sales is only as trustworthy as the franchisee's sales reporting — which is why **royalty verification is the sector's defining commercial dispute**, not an edge case. The same structure appears as lapse-based clawback in insurance, and as reported-volume disputes in agency logistics.

---

## Why now

**Two curves crossed.**

1. Capital costs and efficiency pressure made variable commercial cost structurally **preferred**.
2. Multi-party transactions and metered/rolling revenue made it structurally **harder to verify**.

> **The gap between "we want variable" and "we can verify variable" is at its widest right now — and Reven's product is precisely the closing of that gap.**

---

## Strategic implication

This is the cleanest one-sentence articulation of why the company exists, and it belongs in the deck verbatim:

> ### Variable commercial cost scales only as far as its verification does. Reven is the verification.

### The pricing corollary

Because customers are buying *verified variable cost*, **Reven must not itself be an unverifiable variable cost.**

A percentage take-rate on counterparty money would make Reven **the exact thing its customers are trying to control** — and it is the model finance buyers most resent (impact.com ~2.5%, PartnerStack-style take-fees, MED). Refusing it is not squeamishness; it is **positional consistency**, and it is the neutrality that permits Reven to sit *between* two counterparties.

> *A referee who takes a cut of the score is not a referee.*

**Reven's answer is to price on something with no relationship whatsoever to the counterparty's money:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage 1 — land** | **SAR 50 per seat per month**, published and self-serve |
| **Stage 2 — expand** | Enterprise contracts for the partnership intelligence layers *(price not yet set)* |
| **Stage 3 — settle** | Flat per-payout fees, **capped, fenced and separately reported** |
| **Never** | A visible percentage of the counterparty's money, at any stage |

The same logic supports Sharia-structured fees (**Ju'ala / Wakala**) — a fee for a defined service rather than a share of another party's revenue. That is the same idea expressed in a different legal tradition, not a compliance afterthought.

It also protects the valuation: fixed recurring software is valued as software; transaction-linked revenue is valued as payments, at materially lower multiples.

---

## Operational implication

- **Make the rule engine the place agreements become executable:** tiered rates, caps, floors, accelerators, clawbacks, netting, protection windows, and effective-dating with full version history.

  > **Any commercial term that cannot be expressed as a rule will be settled in email — and every term settled in email is a record Reven does not own.**

- **Support clawback-by-netting.** Refunds, cancellations, policy lapse and churn are where variable-cost trust actually breaks. **Lapse-based commission clawback is a first-class insurance requirement, not an edge case** — a system that only handles the happy path gets abandoned.
- **Keep the eligibility preview prominent.** The *"what will I owe, and why"* screen is the single most persuasive artifact for a finance buyer, and it is already built.
- **Enforce the guardrail in the product:** no visible percentage of counterparty money in the software tier; settlement fees flat and separately reported.

---

## What would falsify it

If buyers **happily accept a percentage-of-payout price**, the contract-value ceiling is higher than modelled and the pricing architecture is leaving money on the table.

Test it explicitly in pilot negotiations rather than assuming the resentment — **but weigh a positive answer against the neutrality cost before acting on it.** The take-rate is a one-way door: it is far easier to add later than to remove once a customer has seen it.
